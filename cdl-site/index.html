Which tests do I need?

Texas issues three CDL classes plus endorsements. What you must pass depends on the vehicle you intend to drive and what you intend to haul in it.

If you want to drive…ClassKnowledge exams required
A tractor-trailer or any combination over 26,001 lbs with a towed unit over 10,000 lbs Class A General Knowledge, Combination Vehicles, and Air Brakes if the vehicle is air-brake equipped
A single heavy truck or bus over 26,001 lbs (dump truck, box truck, transit bus) Class B General Knowledge, plus Air Brakes if the vehicle is air-brake equipped
A smaller vehicle carrying 16+ passengers or placarded hazardous materials Class C General Knowledge, plus the Passenger or Hazmat endorsement exam
Pull two or three trailersT endorsementDoubles & Triples
Haul liquid or gas in bulk over 1,000 gallonsN endorsementTank Vehicles
Haul placarded hazardous materialsH endorsementHazardous Materials, plus a TSA background check and fingerprints
Drive a bus designed for 16+ people including the driverP endorsementPassenger Transport
Drive a school busS endorsementPassenger Transport and School Bus, plus a background check and a skills test in a bus

About the air brake restriction. If you skip the Air Brakes exam — or take your skills test in a vehicle without air brakes — Texas places an “L” restriction on your license and you may not legally drive an air-brake vehicle. For most commercial work in North Texas, that restriction ends the job before it starts. Take the test.

How the Texas exam works

Scoring

Every CDL knowledge exam in Texas is multiple choice and requires 80% correct to pass. The General Knowledge exam is 50 questions, so you need 40 right. Endorsement exams are shorter — typically 20 to 30 questions — and the same 80% applies.

The practice tests here use the same question counts and the same passing bar.

Before you go to DPS

  • Complete Entry-Level Driver Training with a registered provider and have it posted to the federal registry
  • Hold a valid Texas driver license and be at least 18 (21 for interstate or hazmat)
  • Carry a valid DOT medical examiner’s certificate
  • Bring proof of identity, Social Security number, and Texas residency
  • Pass the knowledge exams, then hold your CLP at least 14 days before the skills test

How to use these tests

The source material

Every question on this site is written from the Texas Commercial Motor Vehicle Drivers Handbook published by the Texas Department of Public Safety, which is the document the real exam is built from. Each test card names the handbook sections it covers. Download the current handbook free from the Texas DPS website and read the sections alongside these tests — that combination is what actually gets people through on the first attempt.
